Coefficient source

Wilks uses the 2020 revised (Wilks2) polynomial with a 600 multiplier; Wilks and DOTS clamp bodyweight to their official ranges. IPF GL supports classic/equipped powerlifting and bench-only events. DOTS uses the published DOTS coefficients. IPF GL uses the published Goodlift coefficients (GL = total × 100 / (A − B·e−C·bw)). Bodyweights are converted to kilograms. Federations periodically update or add rulesets (separate raw/equipped tables, weight-class adjustments). Verify against current federation documentation before using a score for qualification or records.

Where the formulas come from

  • Epley (1985): 1RM = w × (1 + reps/30)
  • Brzycki (1993): 1RM = w × 36 / (37 − reps)
  • Lombardi, Lander, Mayhew, O'Conner, Wathan: published rep-max equations
  • Wilks 2020: Robert Wilks' revised coefficients, multiplier 600
  • DOTS: 500 ÷ 4th-degree polynomial of bodyweight (used by OpenPowerlifting)
  • IPF GL Points: 100 × total ÷ (A − B·e−C·bw), official IPF 2020 coefficients per event
  • RPE chart: Tuchscherer / Helms reps-in-reserve model
  • Masters age factors: approximate, based on McCulloch / Foster tables

Calculator Settings

Storage

Preferences and training history are stored in your browser's localStorage. Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Shareable links never include training history.

About the formulas

All seven 1RM formulas are linear in the lifted weight, which allows exact inversion for the rep-max and percentage tables. For 1 repetition every formula returns the lifted weight. Estimates are least reliable above roughly 10 repetitions.

About your data

All calculations, storage, and exports happen in your browser — nothing is sent to a server. An internet connection is only needed for optional extras such as the QR code.
